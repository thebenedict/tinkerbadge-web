## Avoid defensive writing and redundant qualifications

Write for a reasonable reader who understands the document’s purpose and surrounding context.

- State qualifications once. Do not repeat a distinction already conveyed by the wording, heading, or surrounding text.
- Add a caveat or clarification only when it supplies information that changes how the reader should interpret or act on the content, or prevents a plausible, consequential misunderstanding.
- Do not append disclaimers merely to rule out unlikely interpretations. Trust the reader to understand ordinary implications of words such as “proposed,” “draft,” and “example.”
- Keep specific uncertainties, limitations, and implementation details when they matter to the reader’s decisions.

During revision, ask: “If I remove this qualification, will a reasonable reader lose useful information or likely misunderstand the claim?” If the answer is no, remove it.

Example:

Avoid:
“This section collects proposed attendee, organizer, and badge journeys. It can grow as further interactions are defined; the flows below describe intended behavior, not implemented capabilities.”

Prefer:
“This section collects proposed attendee, organizer, and badge journeys. It can grow as further interactions are defined.”

“Proposed” already conveys the qualification.

## Use straightforward, descriptive copy

Write plainly and directly, especially in website headings, labels, and instructions. Describe what something is, what it does, or what the reader can do.

- Avoid quippy marketing language: slogans, forced punchlines, dramatic fragments, and vague promises. Use concrete details and useful instructions.
- Avoid rhetorical contrasts such as “not X, but Y” unless the distinction helps the reader understand something.
- Keep a natural tone and room for personality. Do not force cleverness or talk down to the reader.

During revision, ask: “Does this text help the reader understand or do something?” Remove lines that only add atmosphere or announce the product’s importance.

Example:

Avoid: “When a name isn’t enough. Follow the evidence.”

Prefer: “Compare catalog records.”

## Use numbering sparingly

Omit numbers when the visual layout already makes the order clear. This should be the default in most cases.

- Avoid decorative `01`, `02`, `03` labels on navigation tabs, sections, cards, or other items.
- Use position, spacing, and descriptive labels to make the structure clear.
- Reserve explicit numbering for rare cases where it genuinely helps the reader follow a necessary sequence.

Example:

Avoid: `01 Overview` / `02 Schematic` / `03 PCB`

Prefer: `Overview` / `Schematic` / `PCB`
