# AI Tinkerers Badge Project Specification

Version 0.6 · 9 November 2026 · Working draft for humans and implementation agents

## Purpose and scope

Build an open, reusable meetup badge that identifies attendees, helps people discover shared interests, and provides a platform for playful hardware and software experiments. The development process is also part of the project: show how agents turn a readable specification into validated electronics, an enclosure, firmware, and reproducible manufacturing and web assets.

This document consolidates the discussion, not a frozen design. **Goals** describe the intended experience. **Working choices** are starting points to validate. **Options** remain exploratory. No final parts, battery capacity, dimensions, budget, quantity, manufacturing process, or procurement are approved here. The BOM will be produced later through the pipeline below.

## Current schematic snapshot

The KiCad development schematic now implements the reviewed [project pinouts](pinouts.md). [Schematic implementation](design/implementation_status.md) records the current electrical choices and supersedes conflicting candidate details below. The [10 November design review](design/design_review_2026-11-10.md) enabled charging with an on-board thermistor, removed the battery-ADC switch and display buffer, moved the SAO socket to the front and completed footprints and routing. Prototype and physical acceptance gates remain open.

### Approved first-prototype LED supply

Use one nominal **3.3 V TPS63802 rail** for the ESP32, display/sensors and eight WS2812C-2020-V6 LEDs. Keep local decoupling and sufficient shared regulator capacity. Do not fit the previously considered higher primary rail plus logic LDO.

The user accepted this simpler prototype approach on 9 November 2026. The LED's specified minimum remains 3.3 V; tolerance and transients can fall below it. This is a documented prototype experiment, not a guaranteed lower-voltage specification. Measure startup, low battery, Wi-Fi, display and full-white LED transitions at the load pins, including simultaneous current demand. Eight full-white pixels are approximately 120 mA nominal before overhead and other loads. Revisit the supply or LED choice if those tests show unreliable behavior. Production qualification remains a separate gate.

## Experience and operation

- **Identification:** E-paper shows a prominent name and one or two supporting lines, such as organization or current project. Test real names and viewing distances; adapt typography for long names.
- **Ownership:** Support both a loaner pool returned after each meetup and personal badges bought at cost. Speakers keeping their badges is a proposed incentive.
- **Check-in:** Associate a physical badge with an attendee, apply their chosen display content, role, affinities, and sharing preferences. Integrate with the existing AI Tinkerers platform where practical; the exact interface is TBD.
- **Check-out:** Returning a loaner to the dock should support return registration and charging. Clear its attendee association, local personal data, and persistent e-paper image before reassignment. Detect dock presence separately from USB power. USB charging must not trigger checkout or clear attendee data. Power contacts alone do not identify a badge or confirm a return; define the identification and communication mechanism separately.
- **Fleet operation:** Include provisioning, firmware version tracking, recovery, battery/charging status, and a practical lost, failed, or unreturned badge workflow. Personal badges may retain owner preferences. Exact backend and offline behavior remain open.

## Events and interactions

This section collects proposed attendee, organizer, and badge journeys. It can grow as further interactions are defined; the flows below describe intended behavior, not implemented capabilities.

### Arrival: email link, scan, and badge assignment

**Proposed journey:** An attendee opens a personalized, time-limited link from their event email, without a separate login. On that same page, they choose **Scan Badge** and use the phone camera to scan the QR code displayed on an unassigned badge. The backend identifies the attendee from the link/session and the badge from its code, records event check-in, and assigns the badge. The badge then securely fetches the attendee-approved name/profile content and updates its display; the page confirms progress and completion.

- **Platform dependency:** This assumes modifications to the AI Tinkerers backend and event page, plus a defined badge provisioning, connectivity, and content-fetch interface. Verify the existing check-in model and available APIs before implementation. Scanning a generic badge QR alone does not identify the attendee.
- **Fallbacks:** Show a human-readable badge code for entry on the same page when camera scanning fails. Retain the existing organizer check-in path for attendees without a badge or usable phone/link. Handle expired links, already-assigned badges, duplicate scans, and offline or slow badge updates with clear status and organizer-assisted recovery; do not silently reassign another attendee's badge.
- **Security and privacy:** Treat the email link as a credential: use short-lived, event-scoped tokens, validate them server-side, and avoid exposing attendee data in QR codes or logs. Use appropriately scoped badge-claim codes, expiry/replay protection, and rate limits for manual entry. Make assignment atomic so simultaneous scans cannot claim the same badge. Opening or previewing the email link alone must not check in or assign a badge. Limit content delivery to the assigned badge and the attendee-approved display fields; check-in does not grant contact-sharing consent.
- **Prototype checks:** Test camera permissions, denied-camera/manual-code recovery, QR readability on e-paper, and email in-app browsers as well as normal iOS/Android browsers. Provide an open-in-browser route where needed. Verify that retries do not create duplicate check-ins or assignments and that the attendee can distinguish successful assignment from a display update still pending.

### Further journeys to define

Add personal-badge reuse, profile/display changes, intentional contact exchange, loaner return/reset, and organizer recovery flows here as decisions are made, keeping them consistent with the operating and privacy requirements elsewhere in this document.

## Hardware blocks

### Display and processor

- **Display goal:** Rectangular e-paper with roughly a three-inch visible long edge, allowing some flexibility. Monochrome is the leading direction; colored LEDs provide the immediate color cues.
- **Candidate:** Waveshare 3.7-inch monochrome, 480 × 280. Its visible area is approximately 81 × 47 mm, but its glass is approximately 93 × 55 mm, before the enclosure. The diagonal product name is not the badge width. Compare smaller alternatives and make a full-size mockup before fixing the outline.
- **Integration:** Put the required display support/voltage-generation circuitry on the badge PCB, following the exact panel reference. A raw panel may already contain its display controller; this is separate from the board support circuit and firmware driver.
- **Interface:** The candidate uses SPI plus command/data, reset, and busy signals, with a 24-pin, 0.5 mm-pitch FPC connection. Verify the exact panel revision, connector contact side, pin numbering, flex route, footprint, and assembly availability. Similar connector dimensions alone do not establish compatibility.
- **Driver:** Validate full and partial updates, ghosting cleanup, sleep, and refresh power on the real panel. GxEPD2 is a candidate with relevant panel support, subject to its GPLv3 obligations. Do not assume another panel variant uses the same driver or waveform.
- **Processor working choice:** An ESP32-S3 module with PSRAM, Wi-Fi, and BLE. Select memory and module SKU after defining firmware, connectivity, GPIO, and power needs; the small display alone does not require PSRAM. Other ESP32 variants or lower-power BLE-focused MCUs remain alternatives if their trade-offs fit.

### V1 sensors and peripherals

The following defines the v1 working scope. Ambient light, motion/orientation sensing, and MCU-programmable NFC are included at the functional level; exact parts remain open. IR transmit/receive and an NFC reader need the choices below resolved before the schematic is frozen.

- **Two physical buttons are required.** Use at least one existing button for wake from storage deep sleep; its GPIO and circuit must support the selected sleep mode. Placement, labels, other short/long-press behavior, and functions remain TBD. Candidate uses include display navigation and explicit contact exchange; retain independent hardware recovery access.
- **Ambient light sensor:** Include a low-power digital sensor, preferably on I2C, to adapt LED brightness to the room. Allow manual brightness override. Optically isolate its opening from the badge's LEDs and IR transmitter, and test with the final enclosure and diffuser. The passive ambient sensor and IR receiver may share a characterized visible/940 nm-transmitting window; the emitter needs a separate optical segment.
- **Accelerometer and orientation:** Include a low-power three-axis accelerometer with motion/wake interrupt support for tilt, shake, and badge-bump experiments. Gravity provides tilt when the badge is relatively still; an accelerometer alone does not provide compass heading or reliable orientation during arbitrary movement. Prefer this simpler baseline. A six-axis accelerometer/gyroscope is an option if prototypes need richer gestures or smoother motion tracking; it still does not provide drift-free absolute heading.
- **Infrared, proposed transmit and receive:** Recommend both an IR LED with a suitable current driver and a carrier-demodulating IR receiver, rather than transmit-only hardware. This allows directional badge-to-badge messages and facing-cue experiments. Start by evaluating a common 38 kHz carrier and a short, collision-aware packet protocol. A demodulating remote-control receiver is not a general raw IR sensor: validate its burst timing and filtering against the selected protocol. Check sunlight and lighting interference, viewing angle, range, multiple badges, and transmitter peak current. IR sensing is not proof of a conversation. Final inclusion and optical placement remain a v1 decision.
- **MCU-programmable NFC tag:** Include a dynamic NFC tag with an MCU interface, preferably I2C, and a tuned antenna. Firmware should be able to update an NDEF record, such as a profile or contact-exchange URL, so a compatible phone can tap the badge. Select field-detect/interrupt capability if useful for wake or UI feedback, and test the target phones and enclosure. Passive RF operation can allow reading the last stored record without badge power, subject to the selected part. Default to a public or limited-purpose link, with controlled write access; do not expose private attendee data or credentials in the tag. A tap does not itself authorize contact sharing.
- **NFC reader, optional pending use case:** A dynamic tag does not make the badge a reader. Two passive tags cannot initiate an exchange with one another. If badges must read external tags or initiate badge-to-badge NFC, add a reader/controller capable of generating the RF field, and validate the reader/tag modes and arbitration. This adds cost, power, antenna/layout work, and firmware. Recommendation: use the dynamic tag for v1 phone taps and defer an onboard reader unless one of those use cases is essential; BLE with button confirmation or IR can support badge-to-badge experiments. The proposed ESP32-S3 needs external NFC hardware for either approach.
- **BLE and Wi-Fi:** Retain the processor's radios for discovery/proximity experiments, provisioning, updates, and event connectivity. Evaluate scanning, advertising, power, and coexistence under event conditions. BLE signal strength is only an approximate proximity cue.
- **Display and lighting:** Retain the e-paper display, top role bar, and independently controlled affinity indicators described below. Budget LED drivers/channels, dimming, and power explicitly; the exact indicator count remains open.
- **Battery and power sensing:** Retain battery-voltage monitoring, charger status, separate USB and dock presence sensing, low-battery behavior, and battery-temperature protection appropriate to the chosen cell and charger. Prefer an inexpensive voltage/status baseline; a fuel-gauge IC is a suggested upgrade if usable state-of-charge estimates or fleet charging diagnostics justify it. Voltage alone is not an accurate remaining-runtime measurement.

### Supporting interfaces and optional additions

**Recommended supporting interfaces:** Keep USB-C charging, pogo-pin dock contacts, and manufacturing/test access. Add USB data for firmware loading, diagnostics, and recovery if the selected MCU/module routing permits it, with accessible reset/boot or equivalent recovery access. Provide distinct dock and USB presence signals, and choose the dock data/identification path; charging contacts alone do not confirm which badge was returned. These implementation choices must be budgeted alongside the sensors.

Other suggestions, not additional v1 requirements:

- **Expansion connector or pads:** Expose a clearly documented, current-limited or appropriately protected power rail, ground, I2C, and a few spare GPIOs for attendee experiments. Choose pads or a connector to fit the enclosure and available pins; do not leave boot-critical pins casually exposed as user I/O.
- **Haptic motor or small buzzer:** Consider a simple confirmation cue for successful exchange or errors when the display is slow to update. Haptics may suit a noisy meetup better; both add drive circuitry and power demands. Test whether feedback is noticeable on a lanyard.
- **Capacitive touch:** An optional playful input, after the two-button interface works. Validate sensitivity and false triggers through the enclosure and when worn.
- **Microphone:** An opt-in research variant for on-device sound-level or speech-activity experiments, not a baseline populated part. Require explicit consent, clear enabled-state indication, and no raw audio recording or transmission by default.
- **QR code:** A useful no-extra-hardware fallback to NFC for a public profile or exchange link on the display. Verify scanability within the available display layout.
- **Defer unless a specific experiment needs them:** UWB ranging, a compass/magnetometer, environmental temperature/humidity sensors, camera, and GPS. They add cost, power, layout, privacy, or calibration work. Body heat and self-heating also make a worn badge a poor ambient-temperature reference without careful design.

Before selecting parts, produce one GPIO/interface and power budget covering SPI display signals, I2C addresses/pull-ups, interrupts and wake sources, IR timing, USB, LEDs, buttons, NFC, debug, and dock connections. Check sleep leakage, peak loads, sensor placement, radio/NFC antenna clearance, and recovery access. Validate each included sensor and phone-tap flow on a prototype before freezing the v1 BOM.

### Lights and optics

- **Power status:** Include one separate tiny red/green indicator, owned by the power block and distinct from the role/affinity lights. Prefer 0402 or similar; the currently sourced working candidate is a four-pad 0603 part. See the power-status contract below.
- **Top role bar:** A thin, continuous-looking colored light identifies attendee, organizer, speaker, or another event-defined role. Explore a diffused LED strip/light guide; no specific filament or LED construction is selected.
- **Affinity indicators:** Approximately three to five separately controlled color indicators, likely near the bottom. Five is a prototype suggestion, not a final count. Begin with simple, distinguishable colors rather than combinations or subtle blinking codes.
- **Affinity assignment:** Support attendee-selected interests and optional agent-proposed groups based on voluntarily shared interests. Proposed flow: organizer reviews the event’s categories, attendees confirm or edit their assignments, and the website or display explains the colors. More specific introductions and “can help with / looking for” matching are optional extensions.
- **Appearance:** Soft, modest-brightness glows rather than exposed hotspots. Prototype frosted windows or strips, LED-to-diffuser spacing, and internal light barriers. A single diffuser behind separate openings may simplify construction. Preserve a non-color explanation for accessibility.

## Power and docking

**Target:** At least four hours of representative meetup use, including moderate radio activity, occasional display updates, and LEDs. Define the test workload and measure a prototype before choosing capacity. Also validate low-power storage between events.

Working architecture:

`USB-C 5 V or dock 5 V → input protection and source selection → charger with power path → battery and regulated badge supplies`

- Use a rechargeable single-cell lithium battery as the working approach. Select a documented cell with suitable protection, charge/discharge limits, temperature monitoring, and mechanical protection.
- Each badge needs its own charging circuit and power-path management so it can operate while charging; verify charge termination under load. Charger limits, NTC temperature protection, and battery protection must work autonomously during boot, firmware failure, and sleep. A battery protection circuit is not a charger.
- Prevent USB and dock supplies from backfeeding one another. A basic 5 V USB-C input needs appropriate CC configuration and must respect source current; USB Power Delivery is not inherently required for one badge.
- Size regulators and decoupling for radio/display/LED peaks, not only average consumption. Include battery monitoring, low-battery behavior, and the automatic low-drain storage strategy below.
- Provide USB-C for individual charging and exposed pads for pogo-pin docking. Use guided, polarity-safe seating that does not load the display glass. Protect exposed contacts and each dock slot against shorts and faults.
- Start with one proven slot, then scale a printed/adapted tray. A roughly 20-badge dock was discussed as an example, not a fixed fleet quantity. Size its certified external supply, distribution, wiring, thermal limits, and slot protection from measured operating plus charging loads. Do not parallel badge batteries or assume an ordinary USB-C supply can power the whole tray.

### Automatic power behavior

**Baseline:** Automatic sleep and wake, with no dedicated everyday power slide switch. Keep charging and battery protection independent of firmware. Prototype measurements will determine whether a deeper hardware-off mode is needed later.

- **Event operation:** Run the display, buttons, lights, and required radios/peripherals during the event. Use ordinary idle, modem-sleep, or light-sleep where compatible with the active features. Event connectivity must not be described as storage deep sleep.
- **Dock or USB power:** Allow normal operation, configuration, synchronization, and charging together within the input/thermal budget. External power does not by itself require continuous full activity. A confirmed loaner return can start checkout and cleanup; ordinary USB charging cannot. Distinguish USB presence, dock presence, and the source actually supplying the badge, including when both are connected.
- **Housekeeping before storage:** Finish or safely defer pending writes, save required state, and complete authorized loaner cleanup, including the persistent e-paper image, before reporting return/reset as complete. Apply the defined event-end/inactivity policy, stop radios, blank all LEDs, and put unused peripherals and accessory ports into their low-power states before entering deep sleep. Exact timeouts and event-end triggers remain firmware decisions; inactivity alone must not erase an assigned badge.
- **Storage deep sleep:** Keep only the MCU's required retention/wake domain and necessary support circuitry active. The baseline leaves the MCU supply available for wake; deep sleep is not battery isolation. Wi-Fi and BLE are off, including BLE advertising and scanning. All role, affinity, and power-status LEDs are off, even while charging. The e-paper can retain its last approved image without refresh. Charger, NTC, and pack protection continue independently.

**Wake and recovery:** At least one of the two existing user buttons wakes the badge. Optional USB/dock insertion wake requires a protected, correctly biased signal on hardware that can wake from the chosen sleep mode; USB data activity or a normal GPIO interrupt is not sufficient by assumption. Confirm the selected ESP32-S3 pin, retained domain, polarity, and level/edge behavior, including an input already present at sleep entry. Motion wake is an optional event convenience and must be disabled for storage/transport to avoid repeated wakes. Keep EN/reset and BOOT recovery accessible without working application firmware. Provide a serviceable true power-cycle path, such as access to the battery connector with USB, dock, and any back-powering accessories removed; reset alone is not a full power cycle.

**Source sensing and hardware still to resolve:** Sense USB and dock independently before source selection, or use another reviewed presence mechanism. Combined charger power-good/charging status and a mux's selected-source indication do not prove that both inputs are separately present. Final sensing/isolation circuitry, optional insertion wake, GPIO allocation, peripheral shutdown sequencing, and safe low-voltage sleep/restart behavior remain schematic/firmware work. Do not connect 5 V directly to MCU inputs or let sensing paths back-power a sleeping or unpowered domain.

**Measure before adding deeper off:** Measure complete-badge current during event use, ordinary idle, housekeeping, and storage deep sleep, with and without external power and accessories. Include charger/regulator quiescent draw, battery sensing, NFC, sensors, LED standby, switches, pull-ups, and all leakage/backfeed paths. Set an acceptable between-events storage duration and current budget from those results. Add regulator shutdown, a ship-mode circuit, or battery isolation only if the measured baseline needs it, with reviewed wake, recovery, and charging behavior. No new shutdown IC or battery-isolation topology is selected here.

### Power-status indicator

Include **one separate red/green LED** in the power section of the design and BOM. Working candidate: **Broadcom HSMF-C165 / LCSC C188726**, a **1.6 × 0.8 × 0.5 mm four-pad 0603** red/yellow-green LED. This is slightly larger than 0402, not a two-pad 0603 footprint. The two dies are independent: green anode/cathode pins 1/3 and red anode/cathode pins 2/4. Join cathodes to GND on the PCB and source each anode from a separate MCU 3.3 V GPIO through its own current-limit resistor. Start around 0.5–1 mA per active color and qualify visibility through the enclosure. No driver IC is needed for this low-current baseline. [Manufacturer datasheet](https://docs.broadcom.com/doc/AV02-0584EN).

After firmware starts, show **green for normal on-state operation** and **red instead of green for sustained low battery during battery-powered use**. With valid USB/dock input, green means externally powered, not fully charged; report charging/fault details separately. Both colors are off during reset and storage deep sleep, including when external power is charging the battery. Any later MCU-rail-off mode is also dark. Retain the selected state only through normal on-state idle/light-sleep, and verify GPIO retention. This is a firmware-controlled status light, not an independent hardware power-good indicator.

Reuse battery-voltage sensing and charger status. Measure protected BAT through a calibrated, filtered, safely scaled ADC1 path, never raw battery directly into a GPIO. Provisional battery-mode thresholds are **below 3.50 V for 10 seconds**, clearing **above 3.65 V for 30 seconds**; validate these with the selected pack and realistic load/temperature, suppress transient flicker, and re-evaluate after external power removal. Invalid readings must not imply a healthy battery. The warning is separate from controlled low-voltage shutdown and the pack's protection cutoff. `CHG` indicates charging, not low battery.

Reserve **two additional GPIO outputs**, include indicator current in the runtime budget, and test low-current visibility, boot/reset, sleep/off, charging, battery absence, source transitions and hysteresis. Keep the current-limit resistors, pulldowns and ADC support requirements in design notes while routine passives remain omitted from the draft BOM. Detailed wiring, sourcing evidence and release gates are in **PWR-13 and section 1** of `AI_Tinkerers_Badge_Design_BOM_Draft_2026-11-09.md`; schematic/firmware implementation and prototype validation remain outstanding.

## PCB and enclosure

### PCB

**Current placement trial (10 November 2026):** The requested compact candidate is 110 × 80 mm, with front-side SMT, a proposed 4.5 mm raised display, right-edge USB, full-width affinity LEDs, organized circuit groups, an evenly spaced role-LED row on the remaining top edge and a radio-antenna opening extended to the left edge. It is experimental, not RF/manufacturing approved. The glass is closer to the antenna than the recommended surrounding clearance; the NFC coil, flex and exact part heights remain unresolved. The user selected a rear-copper NFC PCB coil with the front and inner layers kept clear; a 10-turn 18 × 13 mm coil is drawn and needs tuning on an assembled badge. Ambient sensing now sits beside the lower IR receiver for a shared passive-sensor window below the glass, above/between D405 and D406. The IR transmitter remains alongside in a separately isolated optical segment. The top role-LED row stays clear; final window transmission, light barriers, field of view and RGB interference remain enclosure/prototype checks. The prior 110 × 100 mm placement is preserved as a fallback. The user reviewed the placement and authorized a first routing pass, including an R2 exposed notch tip and narrow local support corrections. See the [current placement review](design/pcb_placement_review.md), [first routing review](design/first_routing_pass_review.md) and [design review](design/design_review_2026-11-10.md) for results and remaining gates.

Follow the [datasheet-first hardware workflow](design_workflow.md): review source PDFs, record component facts and project pinouts, independently verify critical tables, then create native KiCad schematic, board, project, and library files. These files implement the reviewed design and supply downstream exports. Organize into display, processor/radio, power, inputs/sensors, LEDs, and connectors/test access, with a short spec and explicit pin/net contract for each block.

**Working stackup:** Four copper layers, with a largely continuous ground reference, appropriate power distribution, and outer-layer routing/components. Two layers remain an option after placement and cost comparison. Observe the chosen radio module’s antenna keepout, including restrictions on ground copper, battery, and enclosure materials.

Validate power circuitry, USB/ESD, boot and programming access, connector polarity, return paths, decoupling, display switching circuitry, and sourced footprints. Include manufacturing/test access and coordinate mounting holes, display flex, battery, buttons, lights, and dock contacts with the mechanical model. A transparent enclosure does not imply a transparent PCB substrate.

#### PCB layout and routing guidance

The visible board should look deliberate and orderly, including through the transparent enclosure. These are design goals, not arbitrary hard constraints. **Electrical correctness, safety, RF performance, signal/power integrity, thermal behavior, manufacturer guidance, fabrication/assembly limits, and mechanical/test access take precedence.** Keep reasonable exceptions and explain important trade-offs rather than forcing symmetry or a cosmetic score.

**Placement, composition, and routing goals:**

- **Functional zones and placement:** Arrange related circuitry into readable groups, with aligned rows or columns, consistent orientations, and even spacing among comparable components where electrical requirements permit. Choose alignment by body edge, pad row, or center as appropriate to the group; use clear gaps between groups. Do not spread components uniformly across the board or move decoupling, switching loops, antenna circuitry, or other placement-sensitive parts away from their electrical optimum merely to complete a row. Reserve intentional space for routing, keepouts, assembly, and access.
- **Clean pad entry and exit:** Where feasible, make a trace's centerline meet the midpoint of the chosen pad edge at 90 degrees to that edge, with a short straight approach before the next bend. Evaluate this in the pad's own rotated geometry. For circular or curved pads, use a sensible centered/radial approach rather than inventing an edge midpoint. Fine-pitch escapes, differential pairs, teardrops, thermal connections, unusual pad shapes, and manufacturer-prescribed layouts may need exceptions; never reshape a validated footprint to satisfy this goal.
- **Consistent routing:** Prefer horizontal, vertical, and 45-degree diagonal segments, with consistent corner treatment and clean fan-outs. Keep parallel runs and transitions visually coherent within their electrical spacing and width requirements. Avoid needless jogs, tiny segments, detours, and layer changes. Preserve required return/stitching vias, impedance, coupling, and length constraints; do not add length matching or parallel runs solely for appearance. Nonstandard angles or curves are acceptable where justified, including RF/NFC geometry and rotated-pad escapes.
- **Visual composition:** Use a small set of local alignment axes, coherent margins, repeated patterns for equivalent channels, and intentional open space to make the board's organization apparent. Let major components and user interfaces establish the hierarchy. Symmetry can help matching structures but is not a goal for unrelated circuits. Judge the assembled board in its enclosure, not only the footprint outlines or bare copper.
- **Readable, usable finish:** Use consistent silkscreen text sizes, orientations, and offsets within the manufacturer's limits, with unambiguous references, polarity/pin-1 marks, connector functions, and revision identification. Keep markings clear of pads and visible after assembly where needed for use or service. Check finger, cable, probe, and rework access as well as geometric fit. Balance labels and artwork without fragmenting reference planes or occupying antenna/optical keepouts.

**Placement approval and visual feedback loop:**

1. **Plan and pre-check before routing:** Define functional zones and their interface connections. Place fixed mechanical/optical/RF items and critical local circuits first. Automate checks of usable zone area, actual footprints/courtyards, keepouts, component height, connector access, and plausible escape/routing corridors. Estimate congestion from pad pitch, track/clearance rules, layer availability, reference-plane continuity, and inter-zone connections. Gross area or a ratsnest alone does not prove routability; report assumptions, crowded regions, and uncertainty.
2. **Human placement gate:** Present the proposed zones and placement with labeled top/bottom views, a 3D/enclosure view, the pre-check summary, and unresolved choices. Where composition is uncertain, compare a few viable placements before investing in routing. Obtain human approval before detailed routing. If routing later requires a material change to the approved placement or zone plan, return for review rather than silently accepting a degraded arrangement.
3. **Measure and render:** Keep electrical/manufacturing checks separate from aesthetic feedback. Alongside connectivity checks, ERC/DRC, and refilled zones, produce repeatable top/bottom layer views and 3D views, plus close-ups of dense areas and pad entries. Inspect actual renders rather than relying on a successful export or a DRC pass. A DRC pass covers configured rules, not every electrical concern or visual quality.
4. **Use geometry-aware suggestions:** From native CAD, flag group alignment/spacing variation, unusual orientations, pad-entry offset/angle, needless bends/vias, and route detours. Respect footprint rotation, pad shape, net class, functional groups, and approved exceptions. Scale pad-entry offsets to the applicable pad-edge size and measure actual edge-to-edge gaps rather than treating unlike footprints as identical. Compare relevant before/after metrics and images; choose tolerances for the actual design instead of imposing universal spacing, percentages, or one aggregate beauty score.
5. **Review, improve, revalidate:** Have a visual reviewer identify concrete issues by component/net and location, separate electrical objections from style suggestions, and request bounded edits. Keep annotated examples of approved placement/routing patterns to make preferences concrete. Use reusable alignment, distribution, repeated-block placement, fan-out, rendering, and reporting tools where helpful; review their output rather than assuming automation improves it. After each meaningful change, rerun affected checks and inspect fresh views, including silkscreen at actual size. Retain the best valid revision, document exceptions, and include a human visual review before release.

Implementation references: the [KiCad PCB Editor manual](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/pcbnew/pcbnew.html) covers alignment/distribution, routing, DRC, and 3D inspection; the [KiCad CLI manual](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/cli/cli.html) documents automated checks and exports. Verify capabilities against the installed version. For the working ESP32-S3 choice, follow the applicable [Espressif PCB layout guidance](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-hardware-design-guidelines/en/latest/esp32s3/pcb-layout-design.html) and the exact selected module's documentation before applying cosmetic preferences. These tools and references support the workflow; they do not establish that an agent has achieved an aesthetic layout.

### Enclosure

Create a transparent or translucent, lanyard-worn enclosure that shows the PCB and feels more intentional than a plain rectangular box. The e-paper panel itself is opaque. Protect the glass, flex, battery, and electronics; retain accessible buttons and ports and allow practical assembly and servicing.

- **Selected direction (11 November 2026):** Clear front/back plates with a dark printed frame, exposed fasteners, separate diffuser inserts and a stepped shoulder following the PCB. The user selected this after reviewing three enclosure concepts. The [A1 enclosure](../mechanical/enclosure/README.md) provides parametric build123d CAD, STEP/STL/DXF exports and independent nominal fit checks. Glass support, controls, exact battery, cables, optics and assembled RF remain physical-prototype checks.
- Attach the lanyard to the structure or PCB, not assumed holes in the display. Confirm the exact panel’s permitted support and adhesive regions. Compliant support is a candidate, not permission to clamp arbitrary glass edges or compress the panel.
- **CAD working choice:** Parametric build123d; CadQuery is a close alternative, with FreeCAD or Onshape available for visual design/review. Keep fit dimensions separate from styling and use KiCad’s PCB/component STEP assembly as a reference.
- Export STEP for mechanical handoff and 3MF or STL for printed prototypes. Check fits, clearances, wall thickness, fasteners, assembly sequence, and process-specific rules such as draft before calling a design production-ready.

## Firmware, networking, and privacy

Provide a stable core for display, inputs, lighting, power, identity/preferences, connectivity, and updates. Expose documented interfaces so attendees and their agents can develop plugins without rewriting the badge. Publish original hardware and enclosure designs under CERN-OHL-P-2.0, original firmware, backend and tooling software under Apache-2.0, and original documentation and artwork under CC-BY-4.0. Preserve dependency notices and third-party terms. See the [licensing map](../LICENSE.md).

**Proximity is not proof of conversation.** BLE strength, IR facing cues, UWB distance, and speech activity each have limits. Treat encounter history or mingling visualizations as opt-in experiments, validate them in real groups, and communicate uncertainty.

Intentional contact exchange is a separate feature requiring deliberate agreement. Prototype both self-button confirmation and a lanyard badge bump; handle nearby competing candidates rather than silently guessing. Define exactly what information is shared and with whom.

Proposed safeguards to resolve before deployment: separate consent for discovery, location/encounter collection, and contact sharing; minimize stored/broadcast identity; clear loaner data; define retention/deletion; and avoid raw audio collection by default. Community plugins need capability boundaries, resource limits, consent enforcement, review, and a recovery path. Open development access must not imply unrestricted access to attendee data or production credentials.

## Design, publishing, and production pipeline

1. **Specify and verify inputs:** Maintain this overall spec, block specs, decisions/open questions, and component/pin/net contracts in version control. Follow the [datasheet-first workflow](design_workflow.md) to keep the main-component PDFs in `docs/datasheets/` aligned with the BOM, extract sourced component facts, and independently review project pin assignments before schematic generation.
2. **Implement and iterate:** Generate or edit native KiCad and parametric mechanical source. Carry forward Harvest’s approach: after each meaningful phase, identify repetitive work, extract reusable tools, and use them in subsequent iterations. Reconcile its reusable and experimental branches rather than copying either blindly. Remove assumptions that restrict designs to two layers, top-side components, or no copper zones.
3. **Validate independently:** Separate design-writing tools from verification. Run focused checks during iteration, then a complete release check against one pinned revision. Check schematic/board connectivity parity, ERC/DRC, refilled zones, footprint drawings, mechanical fit, and exported CAM data. Record tool versions, exceptions, review findings, and file hashes. MCP integrations are optional interfaces, not release authorities.
4. **Build the BOM and cost view:** Derive references, quantities, values, footprints, manufacturer part numbers, and fitted/DNP variants from the design, then add supplier/assembly mappings, stock status, alternatives, dated prices, currency, and quantity assumptions. Keep component cost separate from PCB fabrication, assembly, display, battery, enclosure, dock, shipping, and other full-build costs. This spec intentionally contains no selected BOM or price promise.
5. **Generate the public website:** Develop the website later in the separate **tinkerbadge-web** project, following its [web viewer requirements and architecture](https://github.com/thebenedict/tinkerbadge-web/blob/main/web_viewer.md): place the project README and an attractive, interactive 3D badge on the front page, with schematic and PCB inspection in separate tabs or linked pages. Prefer a static site with build-time CAD exports and independently loaded client-side viewers. Let visitors move from overall spec to block specs, corresponding schematics, PCB layout, BOM/costs, and downloadable source. Regenerate visuals from authoritative CAD so they do not drift. A browser simulator and a documented spec-to-build story are useful later extensions.
6. **Package a release:** Include KiCad sources and required libraries, schematic PDF/SVG, manufacturing Gerbers and drills, assembly BOM and placement files, assembly/test instructions, firmware/build instructions, CAD source and STEP/3MF or STL, web models/assets, validation reports, licenses, and a revision/hash manifest. Verify supplier-specific conventions rather than assuming generic exports are order-ready.
7. **Prototype before batching:** Bench-test the selected display/driver and power system; print a fit/optics prototype; validate four-hour runtime, charging and storage, radio/encounter behavior, recovery, contact exchange, and loaner reset. Resolve failures before a larger build.

Future quote or direct-order integration with JLCPCB is desirable, but API availability, access, assembly support, and ordering authorization must be verified then. Generating a production package does not place an order.
